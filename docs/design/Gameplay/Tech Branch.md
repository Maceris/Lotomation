# Tech Branch

The technology track: tiers T1 to T11, from the Stone Age to the Fusion Age, and the systems that run through them. It runs in parallel with the [Magic Branch](Magic%20Branch.md) after Arrival, and both lead into the combined tiers in [Tiers](Tiers.md). Nothing here is decided.

Each tech tier introduces one new mechanic (see the principles in [Tiers](Tiers.md#principles-that-come-out-of-this)), and ends with a **capstone**: a component of the ship from [Story](../Story.md). See [The Ship as the Progress Bar](Tiers.md#the-ship-as-the-progress-bar).

## Tier notes

### T1. Stone Age

- Matches the [Quests](Quests.md) Stone Age tab: wood tools, crafting station, stone tools.
- [Ideas](Ideas.md) says inventory crafting should be minimal and workbenches needed for almost everything. This is where that's taught.
- Kilns, clay, and the first glow baskets (Pern homage from [Ideas](Ideas.md)).
- Hand gathering exists only here and in the next era ([Resource Gathering](../Prior%20Art/Resource%20Gathering.md) suggestion 10).

### T2. Bronze Age

- Matches the [Quests](Quests.md) Bronze Age tab: copper, tin, mortar and pestle, furnace, bronze.
- Machines exist but are fed by hand. The first **steam miner** and **steam macerator** are here in [Quests](Quests.md), so the line between Bronze and Steam is soft.
- First bulk matter separation: crush stone, sift out copper and tin fractions.

### T3. Steam Age

- The point where the game becomes a factory game: belts, splitters, fluid pipes, steam networks.
- Introduces **pressure**: low-pressure (bronze) and high-pressure (steel) steam, like GregTech.
- Coke oven, blast furnace, first steel. Matches the [Quests](Quests.md) Iron Age tab.
- The Iron Age tab in [Quests](Quests.md) currently ends with the Steam Turbine, which is a natural bridge into the Electric Age.
- **Possible split**: the existing Iron Age tab could stay as its own era between Bronze and Steam if the early game wants more room.

### T4. Electric Age

- Power networks, wire tiers, first circuits (vacuum tubes, resistors, circuit boards from [Quests](Quests.md)).
- Introduces **power grades**: a grade 1 machine can't take grade 2 power, and refuses to run (no explosions).
- Electric versions of every steam machine. Steam machines still work, they're just slower.
- First **logic**: Factorio-style wires and gates ([Types of Things Summary](../Types%20of%20Things/Types%20of%20Things%20Summary.md)).
- First reasonable point to unlock the casino and gacha layer from [Mechanics](Mechanics.md), since the player now has surplus.

### T5. Industrial Age

- Oil, plastics, rubber at scale, aluminum by electrolysis.
- The first real **byproduct problem**: distillation gives several products at once, and each needs a use or a sink. Closed loops and recycling start mattering ([Resource Gathering](../Prior%20Art/Resource%20Gathering.md) suggestion 7).
- Market Terminal and Contract Board ([Mechanics](Mechanics.md)) become valuable as sinks for byproducts.

### T6. Heat Age

- **Heat** is the new axis: blast furnace coils (like GregTech's coil heat) gate which alloys can be made.
- Heat recovery: waste heat from one process feeds another. A natural compactness reward.
- Deeper bores become possible because drill heads survive higher temperatures at depth.

### T7. Precision Age

- **Cleanrooms** as a building puzzle: a sealed room with filters, with machines inside that only work while it's clean.
- Silicon refining, wafers, lithography. Circuits jump in capability.
- Introduces quality as a concept, if [Mechanics](Mechanics.md) quality rolls are used.

### T8. Atomic Age

- Isotope separation (centrifuge cascades, which are a great logistics puzzle).
- **Reactor design**: a placement puzzle where layout determines output and heat, like Extreme Reactors or IC2 reactors, but meltdowns throttle and shut down rather than destroying anything.
- **Radiation as a placement rule**: shielding and distance matter in factory layout.

### T9. Information Age

- **Data as a resource**: research data comes from analyzers and is physically moved (data cards on belts, or data cables), like GregTech's assembly line research.
- The **advanced assembler** needs a data recipe to craft, which is earned by researching the item first.
- Programmable logic: computers that control factory sections.
- Optional: a computation branch for players who like programming (but never required, per the ComputerCraft complaints in [Modded Minecraft](../Prior%20Art/Modded%20Minecraft.md)).

### T10. Orbital Age

- The first launch. An **orbital shipyard** that isn't a loaded world: rockets carry parts up, and the shipyard exists as a progress screen plus a view through a telescope.
- Launch logistics: fuel production, launch windows (but no Playing by Appointment, see *Dark Patterns*: windows come often).
- Satellites that give passive bonuses: a weather satellite for solar, a survey satellite that reveals depth composition.

### T11. Fusion Age

- Fusion reactors as multiblocks with startup cost (like GregTech's fusion reactors).
- Plasma as a fluid with special handling.
- Superconductors make power transport cheap, which should open up new logistics problems rather than remove them.

# Power Grades

GregTech's voltage names (LV, MV, HV, EV, IV, LuV...) are iconic: the player always knows where they stand, and the names escalate into jokes (Ludicrous, Insanely Ultimate). Lotomation needs its own set.

How grades work (from [Types of Things Summary](../Types%20of%20Things/Types%20of%20Things%20Summary.md): energy is measured in Joules in a `long`):

- A grade is the **maximum power one machine can draw**. Each grade is **4x** the last, like GregTech.
- Cables, transformers, and machines each have a grade. A machine fed power above its grade **refuses to run** and shows why. Nothing explodes (pillar: don't destroy the player's stuff).
- A higher-grade machine runs lower-grade recipes faster (overclocking), so old recipes speed up instead of going obsolete.
- Short codes (two letters) go on machine labels and cable tooltips, like GregTech's "LV".

## Recommended: flow names

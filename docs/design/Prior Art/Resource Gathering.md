# Resource Gathering

How other factory, digging, and automation games handle raw resources. This is input for the open question in [Ideas](../Gameplay/Ideas.md): *"Resources are generated without patches – need to nail down the mechanics/details"*, read against the [Design Pillars](../Design%20Pillars.md) (*automate everything*, *all resources renewable/infinite*, a compact factory).

Researched October 2026. Numbers are rough estimates from wikis, patch notes, and community threads, not exact counts. Anything marked **(?)** could not be confirmed.

**How to read the "Automation split" column**
- **Renewable**: automatable, never runs out, and needs no attention once built (for example Satisfactory nodes, a Minecraft iron farm).
- **Upkeep**: automatable, but needs an AFK player nearby, moving miners to new deposits, refueling, or babysitting.
- **Manual**: has to be gathered by hand.

## Patterns worth noting

Most games fall into one of five families:

1. **Finite patches, softened late** (Factorio, DSP, Captain of Industry, Techtonica, GTNH early, Voxel Tycoon). These drive outward expansion. Some do it on purpose (Voxel Tycoon, DSP); Factorio softens it with productivity research.
2. **Fixed infinite points** (Satisfactory, Mindustry, shapez 2, Planet Crafter extractor fields, ONI geysers). Nothing runs out, but the *number* of points still caps throughput, so players expand anyway once they need more.
3. **Make it from bulk matter** (Ex Nihilo sieving, Hydroneer dirt, Astroneer soil centrifuge, Create washing). Location barely matters. The risk is a repetitive grind; your own notes call Ex Nihilo "good *once*".
4. **Biological or genetic production** (Mystical Agriculture, bees, chickens, ONI ranching, Factorio Gleba bacteria). A one-time breeding or setup investment, then passive output. Risks: clutter and tedium (your Forestry/Gendustry notes).
5. **Pull it from the void** (Environmental Tech, GTNH void miners, Stationeers Deep Miner, UU-Matter, Fulgora recycling). Late-game, power-gated, and it completely decouples resources from the map.

Processing depth splits cleanly too. Survival-first games stay at 2–4 tiers. Big factory games run 6–10 tiers at roughly 10³–10⁵ raw units per capstone item. GTNH is in its own category at 10⁹+.

## Suggestions for Lotomation: full automation without outward sprawl

1. **Bulk matter → separation as the core loop.** Every block of stone or dirt (or a generated stand-in) carries a *composition* (e.g. granite = 60% silica, 15% iron, 5% copper, trace tin). Crushers, sifters, washers, and centrifuges pull those fractions out over several steps, with byproducts. You'd have no ores at all, just materials, which fits "we aren't adding ores". To avoid the Ex Nihilo grind, make it **machine-first** (no manual sieving past the first hour) and let higher tiers unlock *new fractions from the same input* instead of more of the same.
2. **Composition varies by depth and biome, not by patch.** Borrow Hydroneer's depth tiers: deep stone is richer in heavy metals, volcanic rock in sulfur, sand in quartz. The cubic-chunk worldgen makes this a natural fit. Where you *dig a shaft or place a bore* matters, but a single column can reach every band, so the factory grows **down, not out**.
3. **Bore / deep-miner machines with abstracted output.** Like Stationeers' Deep Miner or a void miner: one stationary machine that "mines" a virtual column below it, yielding the local depth band's composition forever. It never touches real voxels, so nothing needs chunk loading or gets destroyed, which honours "don't destroy the player's stuff". Gate speed and access to deeper bands by tier, and use lenses or filters to bias the output (Environmental Tech).
4. **Infinite environmental inputs available anywhere.** Water pumps, air condensers or separators (nitrogen, oxygen, argon, CO₂), lava taps, geothermal heat, and solar or wind. Rates depend on biome and depth (Astroneer condenser, Stationeers atmospheres) but never run out. Fluid-interaction generators (cobble/basalt-style) give infinite *building blocks* for the "freedom to build" pillar.
5. **Biology as a renewable branch** (it's already on your Tentative list). Greenhouses, tree farms, fungi (glow baskets!), and **bacterial bioleaching**, a real-world technique where microbes pull metal out of crushed rock, in the spirit of Gleba's iron bacteria. Breeding or culturing is a one-time project that then runs passively, which avoids the Forestry problem of constant item clutter.
6. **Grow output per block, not per area.** Tie throughput to machine tier, overclocking or upgrades, and Factorio-style infinite "extraction productivity" research instead of more sources. Satisfactory's node purity and Mindustry's drill upgrades prove that "same spot, better machine" is satisfying. Multiblocks or compact machines can stack density further.
7. **Closed loops and recycling.** Byproducts from separation (slag, tailings, wastewater) feed back into other chains or recyclers: an Arc Furnace (from your GregTech notes) or Fulgora-style scrap recycling. That makes a compact, efficient base the *optimal* strategy, not just a tidy one.
8. **Late-game transmutation / replication.** Scifi tier 7 in [Types of Things Summary](../Types%20of%20Things/Types%20of%20Things%20Summary.md) already lists transmutation. Something like UU-Matter or matter replication makes rare elements available from power alone, so endgame scarcity becomes about energy and logistics, not geography.
9. **Simulate extractors as rates, not entities.** Because extractors never consume real voxels, they can keep running in unloaded chunks as "X per second" records in the save file. That removes the Minecraft AFK and chunk-loading problem entirely, keeps chunk loading low (the reason ore patches were rejected in [Ideas](../Gameplay/Ideas.md)), and makes every source truly "no human attention".
10. **Keep manual gathering as a bootstrap only.** Hand-mining and hand-picking exist for the stone age (tier 1). Every resource should have an automated route by tier 3–4, so you never end up like Minecraft, where ~15% of items stay manual forever.
11. **Put variety into chains, not into the map.** One input stream (crushed stone) can branch into a dozen products through byproducts, the way GregTech's ore processing does, so the number of resource *types* grows without needing more *places*.
